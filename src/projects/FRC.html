FIRST Robotics Competition (FRC): The Real Deal

The FIRST Robotics Competition (FRC) is a global sport where high school teams build robots to compete in a yearly game. Unlike battlebots, FRC emphasizes Gracious Professionalism, highlighting a competitive yet respectful spirit. I was part of team 2046 Bear Metal from Tahoma High School, which had around 80 members divided into subteams focusing on various engineering aspects. I spent my first two years on the design team and co-founded the General Programming Team (GPT) in my third year.

When the annual challenge is announced in January, an intense 8-week period begins, during which we design, prototype, fabricate, and program the robot. As a design team member, I quickly became immersed in CAD work for prototypes and the final robot. FRC's hands-on format promotes experiential learning of core engineering principles in a fun, competitive environment. I learned to collaborate effectively and consider practical design challenges, improving my problem-solving skills.

In my final year, I shifted focus to co-found GPT, which handled tasks beyond robot code, such as managing our website, setting up a VR simulation, and creating our first electronic scouting system. This system was crucial for making data-driven decisions during the drafting process after qualification rounds. We scouted other teams, gathering data to inform our choices. My friend developed an Android app for data collection, while I created a Java desktop application that wirelessly received, stored, and exported the data for analysis in Excel and Tableau. This was my largest coding project and the first time I applied my skills practically. Although the system was imperfect, it laid the groundwork for future electronic scouting after my graduation
